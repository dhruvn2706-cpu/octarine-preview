Sales & Marketing Collaterals

Turning project information into something a buyer can act on

30 Jul 2026·5 min read

Open ten project brochures and nine of them are organised the same way: location, amenities, floor plans, specifications, about the developer. It is the order the information exists in internally. It is not the order a buyer thinks in.

Collateral is a conversation, not a catalogue

A good brochure follows the shape of the sales conversation that already works in the lounge. Watch a strong sales manager for an afternoon and you will hear a repeatable sequence — usually something like: here is the life this address gives you, here is what makes it different from the other three you are seeing, here is the proof, here is what it costs and what happens next.

Build the collateral in that order and it starts doing work instead of sitting on a table.

The kit, and what each piece is for

  • The brochure. The full argument, for the buyer who is seriously considering. Its job is to survive being taken home and read by a spouse who was not at the site visit.
  • The leave-behind. Short, cheap, and designed to be given to everyone. Its job is to be remembered, not to be complete.
  • Floor plate and unit sheets. The most-used and least-designed pieces in the industry. Clear orientation, honest dimensions, consistent scale across every unit.
  • The price and payment sheet. Deliberately plain. Anything that looks designed here reads as something to be suspicious of.
  • The presentation deck. For the channel partner meeting, not the buyer. Different audience, different argument.

Write for the objection

The strongest collateral answers the thing the buyer is quietly worried about. Too far from the office? Show the commute honestly with times. Small developer? Lead with delivery record. Priced above the micro-market? Explain what they get for the difference, plainly.

Ignoring the objection does not remove it. It just means the buyer resolves it without you, usually against you.

One system, many pieces

Every piece in the kit should come from the same identity, the same render set and the same tone. Not because consistency is tidy, but because a buyer comparing four projects registers coherence as competence — and competence, in a market where you are asking for money years before handover, is the actual product.
