Launch

Digital Launch Collaterals

Everything the launch needs online, ready before day one

22 May 2026·5 min read

Launch day traffic is the cheapest attention the project will ever get. It is driven by curiosity, partner enthusiasm and whatever the campaign bought — and it decays fast. The single most common own goal in real estate marketing is arriving at that window with assets still in production.

The pack, in the order it gets used

  • The landing page. One page, one form, fast on a mid-range phone on mobile data. Renders, location, configurations, price band, enquiry. Nothing else.
  • The enquiry flow. Where the form goes, who is notified, and what the buyer receives back within sixty seconds. Test this on the actual phone of the actual person who will answer.
  • Social launch set. Posts, stories and reels sized correctly for each surface. Not one image cropped four ways.
  • The forwardable PDF. Under ten megabytes, readable on a phone, designed to survive WhatsApp. This is the single most-shared asset in the entire launch.
  • The walkthrough film. Sixty seconds, subtitled, watchable with the sound off — because most of it will be.
  • Partner assets. Ready to forward, with copy blocks written for them.

Build for the phone on a bad connection

The buyer opening your landing page is on a phone, one-handed, on mobile data, with three other tabs open. Every megabyte you add is a percentage of enquiries lost. Compress the renders properly, load the hero image first, and be ruthless about anything else.

A slow, gorgeous page converts worse than a fast, good one. This is one of the few things in marketing that is not a matter of taste.

Have the second wave written

Interest decays in about ten days. Plan the content for days four through fourteen before launch day — the construction update, the configuration deep dive, the location piece, the answer to whatever objection surfaces most. Building that on the fly, while the sales team is busiest, is how the momentum gets lost.

Rehearse it

Two days before launch, walk the whole thing exactly as a buyer would: see the ad, tap it, load the page, submit the form, receive the auto-reply, take the call. Every launch we have rehearsed this way has found at least one broken link in the chain. Every launch that skipped it found the same thing — just later, and with real enquiries.
